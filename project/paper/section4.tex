\subsection{Formal definition}
We now formally introduce \emph{spatiotemporal Pauli channels} (SPCs)---classical stochastic processes obtainable by applying the \emph{multi-time Pauli twirl} to a quantum process tensor. This construction maps any quantum, possibly non-Markovian, process onto a probability distribution over trajectories of Pauli operators. It removes all genuinely quantum temporal correlations while retaining classical correlations. We begin by defining the multi-time Pauli twirl.
\begin{definition}[Multi-time Pauli twirl]\label{def:multi-time-twirl}
    Let \(\mathbb{P}^{(n)}\) denote the \(n\)-qubit Pauli group without phase factors. For a \(k\)-slot process with Choi operator \(\Upsilon_{0:k}\) acting on
    \begin{equation*}
        \mathcal{H}_{\Upsilon_{0:k}} = \bigotimes_{j=0}^k(\mathcal{H}_{\inp_j} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\out_j}),
    \end{equation*}
    the multi-time Pauli twirl \(\mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}\) is the CPTP map
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:twirl-upsilon}
        \mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}(\Upsilon_{0:k}) = 
        \frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}^{(n)}|^k}
        \sum_{P_0,\dots,P_k\in\mathbb{P}^{(n)}}
        \Bigg(\bigotimes_{j=0}^k P_j\otimes P_j\Bigg)
        \Upsilon_{0:k}
        \Bigg(\bigotimes_{j=0}^k P_j\otimes P_j\Bigg).
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}
Intuitively, \(\mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}\) independently twirls each timestep’s input–output pair, preserving the causal structure of the process tensor. In the language of higher-order quantum maps~\cite{tarantohigherorder2025}, the multi-time Pauli twirl constitutes a time-local \emph{superprocess} (or super-superchannel). We now summarise its key properties.
\begin{proposition}[Properties]
    The map \(\mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}\) satisfies the following properties:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Validity}: For any valid process tensor \(\Upsilon_{0:k}\), its twirl \(\mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}(\Upsilon_{0:k})\) is also a valid process tensor.
        \item \textbf{Projector}: \(\mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}\) is an idempotent projector (\(\mathcal{T}_P^{(k)} \circ \mathcal{T}_P^{(k)} = \mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}\)) and self-adjoint.
        \item \textbf{Causality}: The affine (causal) constraints are preserved under twirling.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
The first two properties generalise those of standard Pauli twirling for quantum channels, while the third follows from the unitality of Pauli operators (\(\mathcal{E}_P[\mathbb{I}] = \mathbb{I}\)). We now demonstrate that this projection removes all quantum temporal correlations, yielding a classical stochastic process over Pauli trajectories.
\begin{theorem}[Twirling yields a stochastic Pauli process]
    \label{thm:pt-to-spc}
  For any process tensor \(\Upsilon_{0:k}\), its twirl
  \begin{equation*}
    \Upsilon_{0:k}^{\mathcal{T}_P} = \mathcal{T}_P^{(k)}(\Upsilon_{0:k})
  \end{equation*}
  admits the decomposition
  \begin{equation}
    \Upsilon_{0:k}^{\mathcal{T}_P} = \sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{P}^{(n,k)}}\Pr{(\mathcal{P})}\Upsilon_\mathcal{P}, \quad \Upsilon_\mathcal{P} = \bigotimes_{j=0}^k\Pi_j,
  \end{equation}
  where \(\mathcal{P} = (P_0, P_1, \ldots, P_k)\) is a spatiotemporal Pauli trajectory with each \(P_j \in \mathbb{P}^{(n)}\), and \(\Pi_j = \dketbra{P_j}{P_j}\) are the corresponding Pauli Choi operators. Probabilities \(\Pr{(\mathcal{P})}\) form a valid probability distribution given by
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:spc-probabilities}
    \Pr{(\mathcal{P})} = \Tr\Bigg[\Bigg(\bigotimes_{j=0}^k\Pi_j\Bigg)\Upsilon_{0:k}\Bigg] \geq 0, \quad \sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{P}^{(n,k)}} \Pr{(\mathcal{P})} = 1.
  \end{equation}
  The resulting twirled process tensor \(\Upsilon_{0:k}^{\mathcal{T}_P}\) is separable across all temporal bipartitions and corresponds to a joint probability distribution over Pauli trajectories. The twirl removes all genuinely quantum temporal correlations, while preserving classical correlations. A detailed proof is provided in \cref{app:proof-pt-to-spc}.
\end{theorem}
We now formalise this class of processes under the name \emph{spatiotemporal Pauli channels}.
\begin{definition}[Spatiotemporal Pauli channel]\label{def:def-spc}
A \emph{spatiotemporal Pauli channel} (SPC), denoted \(\Lambda\), is a process tensor that is fully characterised by a probability distribution 
\(p_\Lambda(\mathcal{P}) \equiv \Pr{(\mathcal{P})}\) over spatiotemporal Pauli trajectories 
\(\mathcal{P} = (P_0, P_1, \dots, P_k) \in \mathbb{P}^{(n,k)}\). 
Its Choi representation is
\begin{equation}
    \Upsilon_{\Lambda} = 
    \sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{P}^{(n,k)}}
        p_\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
        \bigotimes_{j=0}^k\Pi_j,
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi_j = \dketbra{P_j}{P_j}\) are the corresponding Pauli Choi operators at each time \(j\). All temporal correlations are encoded entirely within the classical distribution \(p_\Lambda\).
\end{definition}
Although SPCs can be defined abstractly as stochastic processes over trajectories of Pauli operators, our formulation derives them directly from the underlying quantum process. This connection endows SPCs with the same rich internal structure as process tensors, enabling a straightforward and efficient tensor network construction.

\subsection{Tensor network representation}
We now construct an explicit tensor network representation of SPCs. Starting from the Pauli superoperator \(P^* \otimes P\), we define the Pauli tensor \(\mathbf{P}\) via the reorder--fuse--transpose (RFT) operation introduced in \cref{sec:pt-tn} as
\begin{equation*}
  \overline{P}_{(x)}^{b'}{}_{a'} P_{(x)}^b{}_a \xmapsto{\;\mathrm{RFT}\;}
  \mathbf{P}^\alpha{}_{\beta x}
  \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
  \valignbox{\input{figures/tensor-pauli.tikz}}
  \equiv
  \valignbox{\input{figures/tensor-pauli-vec.tikz}},
\end{equation*}
where the index \(x\) of \(\dim{x} = 4^n\) enumerates the \(n\)-qubit Pauli operators. Computing the multi-time twirl of a process tensor \(\widetilde{\mathbf{T}} = \mathcal{T}_P(\mathbf{T})\) can then be written as the tensor contraction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pt-twirl-tensor}
  \widetilde{\mathbf{T}}^{\alpha_0\dots \alpha_k}{}_{\beta_0 \dots \beta_k} = 
  \mathbf{T}^{\alpha'_0 \dots \alpha'_k}{}_{\beta'_0 \dots \beta'_k}
  \Bigg(
  \prod_{j=0}^k
    \mathbf{P}^{\alpha'_j\alpha_j}{}_{x_j}
    \mathbf{P}^{x_j}{}_{\beta'_j\beta_j}
  \Bigg),
\end{equation}
where primed indices are summed over. This contraction is graphically depicted as
\begin{equation*}
  \valignbox{\input{figures/process-tensor-twirl.tikz}}.
\end{equation*}
The Pauli tensors contract only over the system indices \(\alpha', \beta'\), leaving the environment bonds \(\mu_j\) untouched. The resulting tensor \(\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}\) is related to the standard Choi representation of the twirled process tensor via \cref{eq:pt-tensor-to-choi}.

A specific spatiotemporal Pauli trajectory \(\mathbf{x}_{0:k} = (x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_k)\) can be sampled directly from the untwirled tensor as
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(\mathbf{x}_{0:k}) = \mathbf{T}^{\alpha_0 \dots \alpha_k}{}_{\beta_0 \dots \beta_k} \prod_{j=0}^k \mathbf{P}^{\alpha_j}{}_{\beta_jx_j},
\end{equation}
reproducing \cref{eq:spc-probabilities}. This expression can be integrated with tensor network sampling algorithms (e.g., MPS sampling) to draw Pauli trajectories for arbitrary process tensors. Alternatively, one may directly compute the tensors encoding the SPC probability distribution from the Stinespring dilation of the underlying dynamics. We formalise this direct construction procedure below.

\begin{definition}[Process tensor MPO to SPC MPS]
  Given a process tensor $\mathbf T$ with Stinespring dilation $\{\mathbf U_{(j)}, \pmb\sigma\}$, the spatiotemporal Pauli channel obtained by multi-time twirling admits an MPS representation with tensors
  \begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{A}_{x_0\mu_{1}} 
    &= \pmb{\sigma}_{\mu_0}\mathbf{U}^{\mu_0\alpha_0}{}_{\mu_{1}\beta_0} \mathbf{P}^{\alpha_0}{}_{\beta_0 x_0} \\
    \mathbf{A}^{\mu_j}{}_{x_j\mu_{j+1}} 
    &= \mathbf{U}^{\mu_j\alpha_j}{}_{\mu_{j+1}\beta_j} \mathbf{P}^{\alpha_j}{}_{\beta_j x_j} \\
    \mathbf{A}^{\mu_k}{}_{x_k} 
    &= \mathbf{U}^{\mu_k\alpha_k}{}_{\mu_{k+1}\beta_k}\mathbf{t}^{\mu_{k+1}}
    \mathbf{P}^{\alpha_k}{}_{\beta_k x_k},
  \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  where the initial environment tensor \(\pmb{\sigma}\) and the final trace \(\mathbf{t}\) have been absorbed into the boundary tensors. The resulting MPS,
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{x_0\dots x_k} =
    \mathbf{A}_{x_0\mu_1}
    \Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1} 
    \mathbf{A}^{\mu_j}{}_{x_j\mu_{j+1} }\Bigg)
    \mathbf{A}^{\mu_k}{}_{x_k},
  \end{equation}
  encodes the joint probability distribution over Pauli trajectories, where \(\Pr(\mathbf{x}_{0:k}) = \mathbf{p}_{x_0\dots x_k}\).
\end{definition}
This construction, illustrated in \cref{fig:spc-mps}, provides a direct method for sampling Pauli trajectories from the joint distribution \(\Pr(\mathbf{x}_{0:k})\) using standard tensor network sampling algorithms.

This approach extends to systems with spatial structure, where the process tensor is represented by a higher-dimensional tensor network. In the 2D case, for example, the multi-time Pauli twirl maps the process tensor's PEPO representation to a projected entangled-pair state (PEPS) representation of the SPC. The constituent tensors of this PEPS are given by
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a}_{(i, j)}^{\nu^{i-1}_j\mu_j^i}{}_{x_j^i\mu_{j+1}^i\nu_j^i} = 
  \mathbf{u}_{(i, j)}^{\nu^{i-1}_j\mu_j^i\alpha_j^i}{}_{\beta_j^i\mu_{j+1}^i\nu_j^i}
  \mathbf{P}^{\beta_j^i}{}_{\alpha_j^i x_j^i},
\end{equation}
yielding a projected entangled-pair state (PEPS) representation of the SPC. A graphical depiction is shown in \cref{fig:spc-peps}.

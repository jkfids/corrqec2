\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Quantum error correction (QEC) is the primary technique for achieving fault-tolerant quantum computation, yet its practical efficacy hinges on the accuracy of the underlying noise models used to design and evaluate QEC protocols.
    \item Most theoretical and numerical studies of the past model noise as stochastic Pauli errors that are independent and identically distributed across space and time. These models have proven remarkably successful, enabling efficient classical simulation and yielding sharp threshold and resource estimates for a wide range of codes.
    \item However, in reality, noise in devices arises from complex physical processes between the system hardware and its environment, leading to potentially structured, correlated, and non-Markovian noise.
    \item While there is a growing body of work analysing correlated noise for QEC, existing approaches are often limited in scope, relying on assumptions such as limited correlations, ideal syndrome extraction or non-circuit-level noise, being highly specific to certain codes and hardware-specific noise processes, or lack a clear connection to the underlying system-environment dynamics.
    \item At the same time, a parallel literature in open quantum systems has developed a rigorous framework for describing general non-Markovian dynamics via process tensors. Process tensors are quantum combs that provide a complete operational description of multi-time quantum processes, encoding all temporal correlations generated by repeated interactions between a system and its environment. Despite their generality, process tensors have seen limited practical applications in QEC context. This is partly because they are intrinsically complex quantum objects---high rank operators whose size grows exponentially with both time and system dimension---and partly because QEC theory and practice reduce continuous quantum errors to discrete syndrome measurement outcomes corresponding to stochastic Pauli errors.
    \item In this work, we show that these two perspectives---microscopic non-Markovian dynamics and effective stochastic noise models---can be unified within a single framework. In this work, we formally introduce \emph{spatiotemporal Pauli processes} (SPPs): a class of Pauli-diagonal quantum combs or higher order quantum maps that serve as the multi-time generalisation of Pauli channels.
    \item Crucially, this is not an ad hoc invention. We show that standard noise-tailoring techniques---precisely randomised compiling and related Pauli twirling---projects any process tensor description of the dynamics onto a stochastic process over Pauli operators allowing for strong spatiotemporal correlations.
    \item SPPs inherit the spatiotemporal generality of process tensors while interfacing directly with QEC's effective description in terms of Pauli faults and syndrome statistics.
\end{itemize}

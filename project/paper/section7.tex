\section{Quantum cellular automaton model}\label{sec:qcamodel}
In this section, we propose a spatiotemporal SPP model. We construct a microscopic two-dimensional coherent quantum cellular automaton (QCA) on a bath, locally coupled to system qubits, plus repeated `classicalisation' via system twirling which induces a probabilistic cellular automaton (PCA) with a nonlinear kernel \(p = \sin^2(k\theta)\) depending on the number of excited neighbours. The result is a physically derived spatiotemporal SPP, that remains efficient to simulate and sample, while exhibiting nontrivial correlation structure and statistical mechanical properties controlled by few parameters.

\subsection{Quantum cellular automaton}\label{sec:qca-details}
We define the microscopic dynamics of the QCA noise model on a 2D square lattice \(V\). At each site \(i\in V\), a system qubit \(S_i\) is locally coupled to an environment (bath) qubit \(E_i\). We impose a chequerboard bipartition of the lattice \(V = V_\mathrm{red} \cup V_\mathrm{black}\), such that nearest neighbours \(N(i)\) of a red site are strictly black, and vice versa. The environment is initialised as a classical mixture of \(Z\)-basis states, completely uncorrelated with the system.

The joint single-cycle CPTP map is defined as the composition of a local environment storm channel (as in \cref{sec:temporalstormmodel}), a two-layer coherent QCA update, and a system-environment interaction. Formally, we define the joint map as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}_{a, b, \theta} \coloneq \mathcal{U}^{SE} \circ \mathcal{Q}_{B\to R}^E(\theta) \circ \mathcal{Q}_{R\to B}^E(\theta) \circ \mathcal{S}^E (a, b).
\end{equation}
We detail the constituent maps below.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Environment storm channel}, \(\mathcal{S}^E(a, b)\)
    
    Each environment qubit undergoes an independent storm process flipping each qubit from \(\ket{0}\to\ket{1}\) with probability \(a\), and from \(\ket{1}\to\ket{0}\) with probability \(b\). The map for each site \(i\) is
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{S}^{(i)}(a, b)[\rho] = \sum_k L_k \rho L_k^\dagger,
    \end{equation}
    with Kraus operators,
    \begin{equation}
            L_0 = \sqrt{a}\ketbra{1}{0}, 
            \quad L_1 = \sqrt{1-a}\ketbra{0}{0}, 
            \quad L_2 = \sqrt{b}\ketbra{0}{1}, 
            \quad L_3 = \sqrt{1-b}\ketbra{1}{1}.
    \end{equation}
    The global channel is the product over sites, \(\mathcal{S}^E(a, b) = \bigotimes_{i\in V} \mathcal{S}^{(i)}(a, b)\).

    \item \textbf{QCA half-step (red \(\to\) black)}, \(\mathcal{Q}_{R\to B}^E(\theta)\)
    
    The QCA update is implemented as a layer of two commuting controlled-\(X\) rotations with the red and black sublattices as alternating controls and targets. For the first half-step with red controls and black targets, we define the unitary
    \begin{equation}
        U_{R\to B}(\theta) = \prod_{i\in V_\mathrm{red}} \prod_{j\in N(i)}
        \left(
            \ketbra{0}{0}^{E_i} \otimes I^{E_j} + \ketbra{1}{1}^{E_i} \otimes e^{-i\theta X^{E_j}}
        \right).
    \end{equation}
    The corresponding channel is denoted
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{Q}_{R\to B}^E(\theta)[\rho] = U_{R\to B}(\theta) \rho U_{R\to B}^\dagger(\theta).
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{QCA half-step (black \(\to\) red)}, \(\mathcal{Q}_{B\to R}^E(\theta)\)
    
    For the second half-step with black controls and red targets, we define the unitary
    \begin{equation}
        U_{B\to R}(\theta) = \prod_{i\in V_\mathrm{black}} \prod_{j\in N(i)}
        \left(
            \ketbra{0}{0}^{E_i} \otimes I^{E_j} + \ketbra{1}{1}^{E_i} \otimes e^{-i\theta X^{E_j}}
        \right),
    \end{equation}
    with corresponding channel
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{Q}_{B\to R}^E(\theta)[\rho] = U_{B\to R}(\theta) \rho U_{B\to R}^\dagger(\theta).
    \end{equation}
    Together, these two maps implement a full QCA update on the environment.

    \item \textbf{Environment-system unitary}, \(\mathcal{U}^{SE}\)
    
    Each environment qubit \(E_i\) couples locally to the system qubit \(S_i\) via a controlled unitary interaction. We define
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:qca-se-unitary}
        U_{SE}^{(i)} = \sum_{k=0}^1 \ketbra{k}{k}^{E_i} \otimes V_k^{S_i}, 
        \quad U_{SE} = \bigotimes_{i \in V} U_{SE}^{(i)},
        \quad \mathcal{U}^{SE}[\rho] = U_{SE} \rho U_{SE}^\dagger,
    \end{equation}
    The key structural assumption is that the conditional unitaries \(\{V_k\}\) are orthogonal under the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:se-unitary-condition}
        V_k V_k^\dagger = I \forall k, \quad \Tr(V_k V_l) = 0 \ \forall k \ne l,
    \end{equation}
    Furthermore, in this work, we specialise to the minimal family of unitaries
    \begin{equation}
        V_0 = I, \quad V_1 = \vec{n} \cdot \vec{\sigma} = n_X X + n_Y Y + n_Z Z, \quad \|\vec{n}\|^2 = 1,
    \end{equation}
    with \(\vec{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3\).
\end{enumerate}
The entire dynamics are thus governed by three parameters; two temporal parameters \((a, b)\) specifying the environment storm channel \(\mathcal{S}^E (a, b)\), and a third spatial parameter \(\theta\) controlling the coherent QCA on the environment.

To obtain the effective SPP, we apply the multi-time Pauli twirl \(\mathcal{T}_P^S\) on \(\mathcal{M}_{a, b, \theta}\) exclusively on the system subspace. Because the maps \(\mathcal{S}^E (a, b)\) and \(\mathcal{Q}_{B\to R}^E(\theta),\) \(\mathcal{Q}_{R\to B}^E(\theta)\) act trivially on the system, the twirl can be `pushed through' to act solely on the interaction \(\mathcal{U}^{SE}\). Defining the twirled interaction as \(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{SE} \coloneq \mathcal{T}_P^S[\mathcal{U}^{SE}]\), we write the effective twirled joint map
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{a, b, \theta} \coloneq \mathcal{T}_P^S[\mathcal{M}_{a, b, \theta}] = \widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{SE} \circ \mathcal{Q}_{B\to R}^E(\theta) \circ \mathcal{Q}_{R\to B}^E(\theta) \circ \mathcal{S}^E (a, b).
\end{equation}
The central mechanism of this model is that, for a system-environment interaction of the controlled-unitary form in \cref{eq:qca-se-unitary}, satisfying the orthogonality condition in \cref{eq:se-unitary-condition}, the system twirl induces an effective \(Z\)-basis dephasing on the environment in each cycle. Explicitly, the reduced environment dynamics can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
    \Tr_{S_i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{SE}[\rho_{S_iE_i}]) = \sum_{k\in\{0,1\}} \ketbra{k}{k}_{E_i} \Tr_{S_i}[\rho_{S_iE_i}] \ketbra{k}{k}_{E_i}.
\end{equation}
This pipeline guarantees three properties for the effective dynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Nonlinear transition kernel}: The QCA step reduces to a PCA with a nonlinear transition kernel, with the probability of a target site flipping given by \(p = \sin^2(k\theta)\) as a function of the number of \(k\) excited neighbours.
    \item \textbf{Environment re-classicalisation}: The twirled interaction applies an effective \(Z\)-basis dephasing to the environment every cycle, re-classicalising the bath and collapsing any coherent superpositions.
    \item \textbf{System Pauli noise}: Conditioned on the environment state, the induced system noise is a Pauli channel with relative weights set by the coefficients \(n_X^2, n_Y^2, n_Z^2\) of the conditional unitary \(V_1\).
\end{enumerate}
This maps the original QCA dynamics to an effective SPP that admits an exact description as a probabilistic cellular automaton (PCA) HMM. Full derivations of the three properties above, and of the overall mapping, are provided in \cref{app:qca-pca-mapping}. We now summarise the resulting PCA HMM dynamics.

\subsection{Probabilistic cellular automaton}\label{sec:pca-details}
The effective spatiotemporal SPP admits an exact description as a classical HMM with a nonlinear transition kernel, where the nonlinearity is inherited from coherent interactions in the underlying QCA. The latent state is the environment bath configuration, and the emission are the system Pauli faults.

Let \(s_{t}(i) \in \{0,1\}\) denote the state of environment site \(i \in V\) at the begining of cycle \(t\). The Pauli emission pattern during cycle \(t\) is denoted by the string \(x_t \in \{x_t(i)\}_{i \in V}\), where each \(x_t(i) \in \{I, X, Y, Z\}\) is sampled conditional on the updated bath configuration \(s_{t+1}(i)\) at the end of the cycle.

Crucially, because the lattice is bipartite, each PCA half-step updates only one sublattice, and the update probability at a site depends only on the (fixed) configuration of the opposite sublattice. Consequently, conditioned on the opposite sublattice, updates are independent across sites within the active sublattice. This eliminates update collisions and renders the model highly parallelisable.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[0.] \textbf{Environment initialisation}: Choose an initial distribution $\pi_0$ over configurations $s_0\in\{0,1\}^{|V|}$ and sample $s_{0}\sim\pi_0$.

    \item \textbf{Storm update}: Given \(s_{t}\), generate the intermediate configuration \(s_{t'}\) by updating each site \(i\in V\) independently as
    \begin{itemize}
        \item if \(s_{t}(i) = 0\), set \(s_{t'}(i) \gets 1\) with probability \(a\);
        \item if \(s_{t}(i) = 1\), set \(s_{t'}(i) \gets 0\) with probability \(b\);
        \item else set \(s_{t'}(i) \gets s_{t}(i)\).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PCA half-step (black)}: Generate the next intermediate state \(s_{t''}\) by updating each black site \(i\in V_\mathrm{black}\), conditioned on the red configuration as
    \begin{itemize}
        \item for each \(i \in V_\mathrm{black}\), compute the number of excited red neighbours \(k(i) = \sum_{j \in N(i)} s_{t'}(j)\);
        \item set \(s_{t''}(i) \gets s_{t'}(i) \oplus 1\) with probability \(\sin^2(k(i)\theta)\), else set \(s_{t''}(i) \gets s_{t'}(i)\);
        \item for each \(i \in V_\mathrm{red}\), set \(s_{t''}(i) \gets s_{t'}(i)\).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PCA half-step (red)}: Complete the PCA step to generate \(s_{t+1}\) as
    \begin{itemize}
        \item for each \(i \in V_\mathrm{red}\), compute the number of excited black neighbours \(k(i) = \sum_{j \in N(i)} s_{t''}(j)\);
        \item set \(s_{t+1}(i) \gets s_{t''}(i) \oplus 1\) with probability \(\sin^2(k(i)\theta)\), else set \(s_{t+1}(i) \gets s_{t''}(i)\);
        \item for each \(i \in V_\mathrm{black}\), set \(s_{t+1}(i) \gets s_{t''}(i)\).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pauli emission}: For each site \(i\in V\), sample the system Pauli emissions \(x_t\) as
    \begin{itemize}
        \item if \(s_{t+1}(i) = 0\), sample \(x_t(i)=I\);
        \item if \(s_{t+1}(i) = 1\), sample \(x_t(i) \in \{X, Y, Z\}\) with probabilities \(n_X^2, n_Y^2, n_Z^2\), respectively.
    \end{itemize}
    The resulting Pauli string \(x_t = \{x_t(i)\}_{i\in V}\) is the noise induced on the system during cycle \(t\).
    \item \textbf{Repeat}: Increment \(t\), and repeat steps 1-4 for as many cycles required.
\end{enumerate}
This PCA describes a driven classical spin system with a nonlinear update rule. The storm process continuously injects and removes excitations locally, while the QCA-derived PCA spread excitations across the lattice in a neighbour-dependent manner parametrised by \(\theta\). The competition between these stochastic perturbations and nonlinear spatial propagations gives rise to non-trivial steady states and spatial correlation structures, which we evaluate next.

\subsection{Statistical mechanical analysis}
Before studying the noise properties of this model for QEC, we first analyse the PCA dynamics from \cref{sec:pca-details} as a statistical mechanical system. In particular, we would like to isolate the effect of the spatial parameter \(\theta\) controlling the strength of the nonlinear PCA update via the kernel \(\sin^2(k\theta)\) on the emergent correlation structure and steady state properties of the system. 

Throughout, we fix the storm parameters to \(a=10^-4, b=0.5\), and sweep the spatial parameter \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\), which controls the strength of the nonlinear PCA update via the kernel \(\sin^2(k\theta)\). We consider lattices corresponding to rotated surface code layouts for distances \(d=9,11,13,15\), with number of sites (qubits) \(N = |V| = 2d^2 - 1\).

To quantify Pauli emission statistics, or equivalently excitation statistics in the environment, we define the time step-wise density
\begin{equation}
    \eta_t \coloneq \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in V} s_t(i),
\end{equation}
and compute statistics of the time series \(\{\eta_t\}\) for different values of \(\theta\). We simulate bath dynamics up to \(t=10^6\), recording statistics only after a burn-in period of \(2\times 10^5\) cycles. We repeat this procedure for 10 independent trajectories. \Cref{fig:qca-statistical} summarises three complementary diagnostics as a function of \(\theta\). The mean density \(\braket{\eta}\), a susceptibility parameter or scaled variance \(N\mathrm{Var}(\eta)\), and a fitted correlation time extracted from the autocorrelation of \(\eta_t\). 

\Cref{fig:qca-statistical}(a) shows the mean density \(\braket{\eta}\) for \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\). For small \(\theta\), the PCA update is weak and the dynamics are dominated by the storm process, which for our choice of parameters \(a=10^{-4}, b=0.5\) has a low mean density of \(\braket{\eta} \approx 2\times 10^{-4}\). As \(\theta\) increases, \(\braket{\eta}\) increases modestly, before undergoing a sharp climb beginning around \(\theta \approx 0.32\pi\). The mean density peaks to around \(\theta \approx 0.5\pi\), before saturating at this value. This behaviour is consistent with an inactive-to-active crossover (or transition) controlled by the spatial parameter \(\theta\), where below a threshold, excitations cannot proliferate, while above it, the neighbour-dependent update sustains a finite density despite the strong relaxation of \(b = 0.5\). The quantitative behaviour is also largely consistent across different lattice sizes, with a slight decrease in the threshold parameter with increasing size.

To probe fluctuations in the density, we compute the variance of \(\eta_t\) as \(\mathrm{Var}(\eta) = (\braket{\eta_t^2} - \braket{\eta_t}^2)\), and report the scaled variance \(N\mathrm{Var}(\eta)\) in \cref{fig:qca-statistical}(b). This quantity is analogous to a susceptibility parameter in equilibrium statistical mechanics. If correlations grow, the density fluctuates more strongly than would be expected from \(N\) nearly-independent sites. We observe a pronounced peak in \(N\mathrm{Var}(\eta)\) at \(theta \approx 0.38\pi\), aligned with the sharp rise of \(\braket{\eta}\), indicating a strong enhancement of fluctuations in a narrow window of \(\theta\). Such a peak is consistent of a critical or near-critical regimes, reflecting highly correlated global dynamics. Over the system sizes shown, the peak is only weakly dependent on size. Nevertheless, the concurrence of the peak with the sharp rise in density suggests that the critical regime is associated with the onset of sustained excitation proliferation, where correlations are maximised.

To quantify memory in the bath dynamics, we compute the normalised autocorrelation function of the global density time series,
\begin{equation}
    C(\tau) = \frac{\braket{(\eta_t - \braket{\eta})(\eta_{t + \tau} - \braket{\eta})}_t}{\mathrm{Var}(\eta)},
\end{equation}
and fit to a decay exponential \(C(\tau) \propto e^{-\tau/{\xi_\eta}}\) over an intermediate lag window to estimate an effective correlation time \(\xi_\eta\). The resulting \(\xi_\eta(\theta)\) is shown in \cref{fig:qca-statistical}(c). Similar to the variance, we find a strong peak at \(\theta \approx 0.38\pi\) up to \(\xi_\eta \approx 120\), consistent with a critical slowing down and a strong enhancement of memory in the bath dynamics. Both below and above this critical regime, the correlation time is dramatically smaller with \(\xi_\eta < 5\).

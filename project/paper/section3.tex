Quantum maps are tensors; when correlations between their indices are sufficiently constrained, they often admit compact tensor network representations. A paradigmatic example is the matrix product state (MPS) description of quantum many-body states with area-law entanglement. The same principle extends to quantum processes: process tensors are high-rank objects whose temporal correlations are typically structured, making tensor network representations both natural and efficient. Accordingly, process tensors have been represented as matrix product operators (MPOs), locally purified density operators (LPDOs), and tree tensor networks (TTNs). In what follows, we present a tensor network construction of process tensors directly linked to the Stinespring dilation. Throughout, we utilise abstract index notation supplemented by a graphical tensor network calculus. A detailed dictionary translating between Dirac notation, abstract indices, and graphical calculus is given in \cref{tab:notations}.
% For vectorisation, we adopt the column-stacking convention \( X \rho Y^\dagger \longleftrightarrow (Y^* \otimes X) \dket{\rho}\).

From the Stinespring dilation, a \(k\)-slot process tensor is fully specified by a sequence of joint system--environment unitaries \(\{U_j\}_{j=0}^k\) together with an initial environment state \(\sigma_E\). For a unitary \(U\) acting on \(\mathcal{H}_E \otimes \mathcal{H}_S\) of dimensions \(d_E\) and \(d_S\), its Liouville superoperator form is \(\mathcal{U} = \overline{U} \otimes U\), where \(\overline{U} = U^*\) denotes elementwise conjugation. In index notation, this reads
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{U}^{e'_\out s'_\out e_\out s_\out}{}_{e'_\inp s'_\inp e_\inp s_\inp} = \overline{U}^{e'_\out s'_\out}{}_{e'_\inp s'_\inp} U^{e_\out s_\out}{}_{e_\inp s_\inp},
\end{equation*}
where \(e_\inp, s_\inp\) (\(e_\out, s_\out\)) label the environment and system input (output) indices, and primed (\('\)) indices mark dual (bra) spaces, with unprimed indices denoting ket space. To make the connection between the Stinespring dilation and tensor networks transparent, we introduce a specialised tensor representation derived from the Liouville form via a \emph{reorder--fuse--transpose} (RFT) operation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reorder} indices to group each ket index with its corresponding bra index.
    \item \textbf{Fuse} each bra--ket (primed--unprimed) pair into a single compound index.
    \item \textbf{Transpose} globally so that time flows from left to right, consistent with standard tensor network diagrams.
\end{enumerate}
Explicitly, for a superoperator \(\mathcal{S}\),
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
    &\mathcal{S}^{b'_0 b'_1 \dots b_0 b_1}{}_{a'_0 a'_1 \dots a_0 a_1} \\
    &\qquad\qquad\xmapsto{\;\text{reorder}\;}
    \mathcal{S}^{b'_0 b_0 b'_1 b_1 \dots}{}_{a'_0 a_0 a'_1 a_1 \dots}
    \xmapsto{\;\text{fuse}\;}
    \mathcal{S}^{\beta_0 \beta_1 \dots}{}_{\alpha_0 \alpha_1 \dots}
    \xmapsto{\;\text{transpose}\;}
    \mathbf{S}^{\alpha_0 \alpha_1 \dots}{}_{\beta_0 \beta_1 \dots},
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
where \(\alpha_j = (a'_j a_j)\) and \(\beta_j = (b'_j b_j)\) are fused indices of dimension \(\dim(\alpha_j) = \dim(a_j)^2\). Latin letters denote basic indices and Greek letters fused indices. Applying RFT to a unitary superoperator \(\mathcal{U}_j\) yields a 4-legged tensor \(\mathbf{U}_{(j)}\),
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{U}_j^{e'_{j+1} e_{j+1} b'_j b_j}{}_{e'_j e_j a'_j a_j} \xmapsto{\;\mathrm{RFT}\;} \mathbf{U}_{(j)}^{\mu_j \alpha_j}{}_{\mu_{j+1} \beta_j},
\end{equation*}
with fused indices \(\mu_j = (e'_j e_j)\) for the environment and \(\alpha_j=(a'_j a_j)\), \(\beta_j=(b'_j b_j)\) for the system input and output, respectively. Their dimensions are \(\dim(\mu_j) = d_E^2\) and \(\dim(\alpha_j) = \dim(\beta_j) = d_S^2\). The mapping is graphically depicted as

where thick wires denote fused indices. The initial environment state \(\dket{\sigma_E}\) is mapped similarly,
\begin{equation*}
    \sigma^{e'_0e_0} \xmapsto {\;RFT\;} \pmb{\sigma}_{\mu_0}

    \valignbox{\input{figures/tensor-env-init.tikz}} = \left(\valignbox{\input{figures/vectorize-sigma.tikz}}\right)^T.
\end{equation*}
To represent the final partial trace over the environment, we include a trace tensor \(\mathbf{t}\), corresponding to the unnormalised Bell state \(\ket{\Phi^+} = \dket{\mathbb{I}}\),
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{t}^{\mu_{k+1}} = \delta^{e'_{k+1}e_{k+1}}
    \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
    \valignbox{\input{figures/tensor-trace-env.tikz}} = \valignbox{\input{figures/trace-env.tikz}}.
\end{equation*}
The full \(k\)-slot process tensor is then constructed as an MPO by contracting the environment indices \(\mu_j\) across all time. This yields a tensor \(\mathbf{T}\) with open system indices corresponding to the inputs (\(\alpha_j\)) and outputs (\(\beta_j\)) at each time \(j\). Formally, we define:
\begin{definition}[MPO representation of a process tensor]\label{def:pt-mpo}
A $k$-slot process tensor $\Upsilon_{0:k}$ admits a matrix product operator (MPO) representation,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{T}^{\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_k}{}_{\beta_0,\dots,\beta_k} = \sum_{\mu_0,\dots,\mu_{k+1}} \pmb{\sigma}_{\mu_0}\mathbf{U}_{(0)}^{\mu_0\alpha_0}{}_{\mu_1\beta_0}\mathbf{U}_{(1)}^{\mu_1\alpha_1}{}_{\mu_2\beta_1}\cdots\mathbf{U}_{(k)}^{\mu_k\alpha_k}{}_{\mu_{k+1}\beta_k}\mathbf{t}^{\mu_{k+1}}.
\end{equation}
Here $\pmb{\sigma}$, $\mathbf{U}_{(j)}$, and $\mathbf{t}$ are tensor representations of the initial environment state, the joint $SE$ unitary at time $j$, and the final environment trace, respectively; the environment indices $\mu_j$ are the MPO's virtual bonds of dimension \(\chi_\mathrm{bond} = \dim{(\mu_j)} \le d_E^2\).
\end{definition}

To recover the standard Choi form, we simply reorder and reshape the fused indices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pt-tensor-to-choi}
    \mathbf{T}^{\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_k}{}_{\beta_0,\dots,\beta_k} \mapsto \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{Choi}}^{a_0b_0\dots a_kb_k}{}_{a'_0b'_0\dots a'_kb'_k} \longleftrightarrow \Upsilon_{0:k}.
\end{equation}
In the tensor network picture, the action of \(\mathbf{T}\) on an initial state and sequence of instruments is realised as a network contraction between the `top comb' process tensor and the `bottom comb' instruments. The initial system state \(\dket{\rho_\mathrm{in}}\) and each instrument superoperator \(\mathcal{A}_j\) are mapped into their corresponding tensors \(\pmb{\rho}^\mathrm{in}\) and \(\mathbf{A}_{(j)}\) via the RFT operation. The final output state \(\pmb{\rho}^\mathrm{out}\) is then given by
\begin{equation}
    \pmb{\rho}^\mathrm{out}_{\alpha_{k+1}} = \pmb{\sigma}_{\mu_0}\pmb{\rho}^\mathrm{in}_{\alpha_0}\mathbf{U}_{(0)}^{\mu_0\alpha_0}{}_{\mu_1\beta_0}\mathbf{A}_{(0)}^{\beta_0}{}_{\alpha_1}\mathbf{U}_{(1)}^{\mu_1\alpha_1}{}_{\mu_2\beta_1}\mathbf{A}_{(1)}^{\beta_1}{}_{\alpha_2}\cdots\mathbf{U}_{(k)}^{\mu_k\alpha_k}{}_{\mu_{k+1}\beta_k}\mathbf{t}^{\mu_{k+1}}\mathbf{A}_{(k)}^{\beta_k}{}_{\alpha_{k+1}},
\end{equation}
where repeated indices imply summation (contraction). Graphically,
\begin{equation*}
    \valignbox{\input{figures/process-tensor-action.tikz}}.
\end{equation*}
This contraction naturally generalises to cases where the instruments are themselves classically or quantumly correlated across time, in which case the product of local instrument tensors is replaced by a more general tensor network.

So far we have considered only the one-dimensional (temporal) structure of process tensors, where each tensor carries dense system indices of size \(d^2_S\). For composite systems---such as spin chains---these indices can be further factorised along spatial degrees of freedom, promoting the MPO to a two-dimensional tensor network with time along one axis and space along the other. For a unitary tensor \(\mathbf{U}_{(j)}\) acting on an \(n\)-partite system, we can perform a series of singular value decompositions (SVDs) along subsystem indices, yielding
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{U}_{(j)}^{\mu_j\alpha_j}{}_{\mu_{j+1}\beta_j} 
    = \sum_{\nu_j^0,\dots,\nu_j^{n-2}}
    \mathbf{u}_{(0,j)}^{\mu_j^0\alpha_j^0}{}_{\beta_j^0\mu_{j+1}^0\nu_j^0}
    \mathbf{u}_{(1,j)}^{\nu_j^0\mu_j^1\alpha_j^1}{}_{\beta_j^1\mu_{j+1}^1\nu_j^1}\dots 
    \mathbf{u}_{(n-1,j)}^{\nu_j^{n-2}\mu_j^{n-1}\alpha_j^{n-1}}{}_{\beta_j^{n-1}\mu_{j+1}^{n-1}},
\end{equation*}
where index superscripts \(i=0,\dots,n-1\) label subsystems (spatial coordinates) and index subscripts \(j=0,\dots,k\) label timesteps. Performing this decomposition for all \(j\) and similarly for the initial environment tensor \(\pmb{\sigma}\), we obtain a 2D tensor network representation of the process tensor,
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{T}^{\{\alpha_j^i\}}{}_{\{\beta_j^i\}} = 
    \sum_{\{\mu_j^i\}} \sum_{\{\lambda^i\}} \sum_{\{\nu_j^i\}}
    \Biggl[\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\pmb{\sigma}_{(i)}^{\lambda^{i-1}}{}_{\mu_0^i\lambda^i}\Biggr]
    \Biggl[\prod_{j=0}^{k}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{u}_{(i, j)}^{\nu^{i-1}_j\mu_j^i\alpha_j^i}{}_{\beta_j^i\mu_{j+1}^i\nu_j^i}\Biggr]
    \Biggl[\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{t}_{(i)}^{\mu_{k+1}^i}\Biggr],
\end{equation*}
with spatial boundary conditions such that left indices \(\lambda^{-1}, \nu^{-1}_j\) and right indices \(\lambda^{n-1}, \nu^{n-1}_j\) are absent. The spatial bond dimensions \(\chi_s = \dim(\nu_j^i)\) are set by the SVD truncation, while the temporal bond dimensions remain bounded by the environment size, \(\chi_t = \dim(\mu_j^i) \le d_E^2\). \Cref{fig:tensor-network-2d} displays a graphical representation of this 2D tensor network.

\section{Temporal storm model}\label{sec:temporalstormmodel}

\subsection{Model}
We now instantiate the general SPC formalism in a one-dimensional, analytically tractable setting, which we refer to as the \emph{temporal storm model}. Each system qubit $S_i$ is coupled to its own two-level environment $E_i$ with classical basis states $\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}$, representing \emph{calm} and \emph{storm} conditions, respectively. For clarity, we focus on a single site and suppress the index $i$ in what follows.

The environment undergoes a time-homogeneous classical Markov chain with transition probabilities $\Gamma_{i\to j}$ between calm and storm (Fig.~X). The system--environment interaction is modelled as a quantum channel conditioned on the environment state. When the environment is in state $\ket{j}$, the system evolves according to
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{E}_j[\rho_S]
    = \sum_x q^{(x)}_j \,\sigma^{(x)} \rho_S \sigma^{(x)},
\end{equation*}
where $\sigma^{(x)}$ denotes the Pauli operators indexed by $x$, and $q^{(x)}_j$ are probabilities satisfying \(\sum_x q^{(x)}_j = 1\). The joint system--environment evolution over a single time step is thus described by the CPTP map
\begin{equation*}
    \Phi[\rho_{SE}]
    = \sum_{i,j,x} K^{(x)}_{ij} \,\rho_{SE}\, K^{(x)\dagger}_{ij},
    \qquad
    K^{(x)}_{ij}
    = \sqrt{\Gamma_{i\to j}\,q^{(x)}_j}\;
      \ketbra{j}{i}_E \otimes \sigma^{(x)}_S.
\end{equation*}
Because the environment dynamics are classical and the conditional system channels \(\mathcal{E}_j\) are Pauli, the corresponding process tensor is invariant under the multi-time Pauli twirl in \cref{eq:twirl-upsilon}; in other words, this construction directly specifies an SPC. (\Fidel{Mention that this SPC represents the image of a family of process tensors under the twirling map?})

Labelling the environment transition probabilities as $a = \Gamma_{0\to 1}$ and $b = \Gamma_{1\to 0}$, the corresponding transfer operator $T$ and SPC matrices $A^{(x)}$ are
\begin{equation*}
    T
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 - a & a \\
        b & 1 - b
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    A^{(x)} = T\,\mathrm{diag}\big(q_0^{(x)}, q_1^{(x)}\big).
\end{equation*}
The spectrum of $T$ is easily obtained with \(\lambda_1 = 1, \lambda_2 = 1 - a - b\). The subleading eigenvalue \(\lambda_2\) sets the spectral gap \(\Delta\) and correlation length \(\xi\),
\begin{equation*}
    \Delta = a + b, \quad \xi = -\frac{1}{\ln(1-a-b)},
\end{equation*}
The left and right eigenvectors associated with $\lambda_1$, corresponding to the left and right stationary states, are
\begin{equation*}
    \bra{l} =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \pi_0 & \pi_1
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \ket{r} =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        1
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}
where \(\pi_0 = b/(a+b)\) and \(\pi_1 = a/(a+b)\) are the stationary calm and storm fractions, respectively. These immediately lead to the marginal Pauli error rates,
\begin{equation*}
    \bar{p}^{(x)}
    = \pi_0 q_0^{(x)} + \pi_1 q_1^{(x)} = \frac{bq_0^{(x)} + aq_1^{(x)}}{a+b}.
\end{equation*}
